  • Tokens are split per conversation. Each turn re-sends everything before it, so only the growth counts as input and the rest is a cache read: 98% of all tokens here. Two things push that low. A conversation already running when the range opened has its first turn counted in full, and a real provider's cache expires between turns where this one never does.
  • GPU hours are wall-clock, with concurrent requests merged. Energy is billed by the clock, not by the request. Cost covers the whole day: 9 W under load (GPU rail only, well under the true figure), 3 W idle (assumed at 30% of load), at 0.170 $/kWh.
  • That wattage is the GPU's own reported draw, which is a floor rather than a measurement: it leaves out the CPU, memory, disks, fans and power-supply losses that serving a token also costs, and on unified-memory boards it covers only one rail of the module. Put a meter on the wall under load and at rest, and set watts and watts_idle from that.
